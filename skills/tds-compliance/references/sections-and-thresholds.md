# TDS sections — matching a payment to a section

> Snapshot of November 2026. Rates and thresholds are set by the Income-tax Act and the annual
> Finance Act and change most years. **Confirm every rate and threshold on
> https://www.incometax.gov.in before use.** The numbers below are indicative, to help identify the
> right section — not to file with.
>
> **Which Act applies:** the Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026. TDS on payments to
> residents is now in **section 393(1)**, as one table. The 192 / 194-series numbers belong to the
> 1961 Act and apply to payments up to 31 March 2026.

## How to read this

Match the **nature of the payment** to a section, then look up the live rate and threshold. "1% / 2%"
style splits usually mean *individual/HUF vs others*. Thresholds are often *per transaction* and/or
*per year* — both can apply.

## Resident payments from 1 April 2026: section 393(1), Income-tax Act, 2025

serial number of the table, for example "section 393(1), Table Sl. No. 6(iii)". The last column is
a cross-reference to the 1961 Act section that covered the same payment.

Points the section itself makes:

- Once the amount, or the aggregate of amounts in the tax year, exceeds the threshold, tax is
  deducted on the **entire** amount, not only the part above the threshold. Purchase of goods,
  serial 8(ii), is the exception: tax is on the sum above ₹50 lakh.
- For contractors, serial 6(i), a works contract that shows the value of material separately is
  taxed on the invoice value excluding that material.
- Winnings, cash withdrawals and payments to a firm's partners are in section 393(3). Payments to
  non-residents are in section 393(2).

## Non-resident payments

- **Section 195** governs most payments to non-residents. Rates, surcharge and cess differ, and a
  **DTAA (tax treaty)** may reduce the rate — but claiming treaty relief needs a Tax Residency
  Certificate and often **Form 15CA / 15CB**. Do not apply a resident 194-rate here. Recommend a
  professional for cross-border payments.

## Rate uplifts to always check

- **No valid PAN (section 206AA):** deduct at a higher rate (often 20%, or the section rate if
  higher).
- **Non-filer of returns (section 206AB):** a higher rate can apply; check the payee's status on
  the income-tax compliance utility.
- **Lower/nil deduction certificate (section 197):** if the payee holds one (Form 13 approval),
  deduct at the certified lower rate.

## TCS (collected on certain sales)

- **206C(1)** — specified goods (scrap, minerals, etc.).
- **206C(1H)** — sale of goods above a turnover/consideration threshold (interacts with 194Q —
  generally only one applies to a transaction).
- **206C(1G)** — foreign remittance under LRS and overseas tour packages.
