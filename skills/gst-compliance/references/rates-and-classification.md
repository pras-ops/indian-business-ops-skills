# GST rates, slabs and classification

> Snapshot written October 2026. GST rates are changed by notification and were overhauled on
> 22 September 2025 ("GST 2.0"). Treat the structure below as orientation and confirm any specific
> item's rate on the GST portal rate finder or the latest CBIC rate notification before use.

## The slab structure (post 22 September 2025)

GST 2.0 collapsed the earlier five-tier structure (0 / 5 / 12 / 18 / 28) into a simpler set:

- **0%** (nil-rated / exempt) — many essentials and specified supplies.
- **5%** — essential and mass-consumption goods and services.
- **18%** — the standard rate for most goods and services.
- **40%** — a special rate for luxury and "sin" goods (e.g. tobacco, aerated drinks, high-end
  automobiles, betting/gambling).

The old **12%** and **28%** slabs were largely redistributed into 5% / 18% / 40%. A handful of
special rates and cesses persist for specific items. Because reclassification was broad, **do not
assume an item kept its old rate** — look it up.

## Finding the rate for a specific item

1. Identify the **HSN code** (for goods) or **SAC code** (for services). HSN follows the
   international Harmonised System; SAC is the GST services equivalent.
2. Use the **rate finder on the GST portal** or the current CBIC rate notification for that HSN/SAC.
3. If the item could fall under more than one heading, flag the ambiguity — classification disputes
   are a common source of litigation. Recommend a CA for a binding view.

## HSN / SAC digit requirement

How many digits of HSN a business must quote on invoices and in GSTR-1 depends on **aggregate
turnover** (more digits required above a turnover threshold). Confirm the current digit requirement
on the portal; getting it wrong is a common return-validation error.

## Place of supply and the tax split

The rate is only half the entry — the **tax type** depends on place of supply:

- Same-state supply → **CGST + SGST** (half each).
- Inter-state supply, import, export → **IGST**.
- Services have specific place-of-supply rules (e.g. immovable property, events, transport,
  telecom) that can differ from the billing address. When a service's place of supply is unclear,
  say so.

## Reverse charge (RCM)

Under reverse charge the **recipient** pays GST instead of the supplier — for certain notified
supplies (e.g. specific services, supplies from unregistered persons where notified, imports of
services). Under RCM:

- The recipient pays the tax in cash (not by setting off ITC), then may claim ITC subject to the
  normal conditions.
- RCM liabilities must appear in GSTR-3B.

Confirm the current list of RCM-notified supplies on CBIC before treating something as RCM.

## Common classification mistakes to watch for

- Treating a **composite supply** (naturally bundled, e.g. goods + installation) or a **mixed
  supply** (separately available items sold together) incorrectly — the rate rule differs.
- Applying a goods rate to what is actually a service, or vice versa.
- Missing that **exempt** and **nil-rated** are not the same as **non-GST** — they affect ITC
  differently.
